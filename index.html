Exploring Australia’s Scenic Lookouts and Walking Destinations

Where are Australia’s scenic views, how accessible are scenic walks, and how do natural attractions align with domestic tourism?

Section 1

Where are Australia’s scenic views?

Australia’s recorded scenic lookouts are unevenly distributed. This section explores where lookout locations are recorded across the country, how their numbers differ between states and territories, and where scenic viewpoints are concentrated.

Where are Australia’s scenic lookouts?

Each point represents a recorded lookout. Hover over a point to see its name and state.

Which states have the most recorded lookouts?

Recorded scenic lookouts are unevenly distributed across Australian states and territories.

Key finding: New South Wales has 401 recorded lookouts—more than three times Queensland’s 122, the second-highest count.

Where are scenic lookouts most concentrated?

Nearby lookout locations are grouped into geographic areas. Larger and darker circles indicate areas with more recorded scenic lookouts.

Section 2

How accessible are scenic walks?

A closer look at scenic lookout walks in New South Wales shows that reaching a great view can range from a short, easy walk to a demanding multi-hour hike.

How does walking time change with track difficulty?

Box plots compare the distribution of typical walking time across track grades. Higher grades generally represent more demanding walking conditions.

Do longer walks always take more time?

Each point represents a scenic lookout walk. Distance and typical walking time reveal how route length and difficulty interact.

Key finding: The NSW case-study sample ranges from short Grade 1 walks of around 0.5 km to a 9 km Grade 5 route requiring several hours.

Which scenic walks demand the most effort?

Distance, walking time and track grade are normalised to a common scale. Darker cells indicate relatively greater effort within this NSW case-study sample.

Section 3

Where is domestic tourism strongest?

Domestic tourism activity is unevenly distributed across Australia. Trip volumes, visitor nights and expenditure reveal different dimensions of tourism activity between states and territories.

Which states receive the most domestic trips?

Circle size represents total domestic trips during the June Quarter 2026. Larger circles indicate higher trip volumes.

Where is domestic tourism spending highest?

Darker states represent higher regional tourism expenditure during the June Quarter 2026.

Key finding: New South Wales records the highest regional tourism expenditure in the June Quarter 2026 at about $11.76 billion, followed by Victoria and Queensland.

How do trips, visitor nights and spending compare?

Each indicator is normalised relative to the highest-performing state. Darker cells indicate a higher relative level for that tourism measure.

Section 4

Do scenic destinations align with tourism activity?

Scenic attractions form one part of Australia’s wider tourism landscape. Comparing recorded lookout locations with tourism activity can reveal whether the two patterns tend to occur together. These relationships show association rather than direct causation.

Do states with more recorded lookouts receive more domestic trips?

Each point represents a state or territory. The dashed trend line summarises the overall association between recorded scenic lookouts and domestic tourism activity.

Key finding: The overall relationship is positive, but it is not one-to-one. Victoria records fewer lookouts than Queensland while receiving substantially more domestic trips.

Do more scenic lookouts align with higher tourism spending?

Bubble position compares recorded lookout numbers with tourism expenditure, while bubble size represents visitor nights.

Which states combine strong scenery and tourism indicators?

Scenic and tourism indicators are normalised to a common scale. Darker cells indicate a higher relative level within Australia.

Conclusion

Scenic landscapes form part of Australia’s wider tourism story

Recorded scenic lookouts are concentrated particularly around Australia’s eastern and southern regions. Scenic walking experiences also vary substantially in accessibility, ranging from short Grade 1 walks to demanding Grade 5 routes. Several states with higher recorded lookout counts also show strong domestic tourism activity, although these patterns indicate association rather than direct causation.

Data sources

Geoscience Australia
Composite Gazetteer of Australia — source of recorded place-name locations. Lookout records used in this visualisation were derived by filtering features classified as LOOKOUT.
View official data source

Tourism Research Australia
Domestic Tourism Statistics (DoTS), June Quarter 2026 — state and territory trips, visitor nights and expenditure.
View official data and XLSX tables

NSW National Parks and Wildlife Service
Individual official walking-track pages were used to compile the NSW case-study dataset. Track difficulty follows the Australian Walking Track Grading System.
View official grading system


Data limitations

Recorded lookout locations represent gazetted place-name information rather than the complete number of scenic viewpoints available to visitors. Walking-track analysis is presented as a NSW case study and should not be interpreted as a complete national comparison of all Australian walking tracks.
